### 1. [Spectrum Adaptation of AI-Native Autoencoder Communications with Transfer Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34942v1)
👤 Yalin E. Sagduyu, Tugba Erpek | 📅 2026-11-25

**详情:** End-to-end physical-layer learning replaces separate modulation, coding, and detection operations with an autoencoder (AE) that consists of a jointly trained encoder-decoder pair at the transmitter and the receiver. This paper studies AI-native communications with AEs and quantifies the sensitivity to training-test mismatch in channel models. Models trained only on an AWGN channel achieve low bit error rates (BERs) under matched conditions but degrade severely on Rayleigh fading with practical front-end impairments. To reduce the time and energy required to adapt to new channel conditions, we propose a low-latency transfer-learning procedure that starts from an AWGN-pretrained system and fine-tunes only a small subset of transmitter and receiver layers while freezing the remainder. We further formulate layer selection as a budget-constrained optimization problem that identifies the most informative parameters to update during adaptation. This preserves the learned signal geometry and the deep decision structure while adapting lightweight front ends to fading- and impairment-induced mixing. For AE communications, we show that partial updates remove the mismatch-induced error floor and approach the BER of fully retrained models. We provide a layer-wise study and quantify resource-performance trade-offs, showing that modest adaptation substantially reduces BER while cutting training time, multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations, energy consumption, and the fraction of updated parameters for resource-efficient implementation.

### 2. [Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934v1)
👤 Charlie Griffin, Oliver Makins | 📅 2026-11-25

**详情:** AI monitors, classifiers that flag dangerous behaviour by AI agents, are often evaluated against transcripts generated by attack policies acting as proxies for dangerous behaviour in deployment. We evaluate an ex-production OpenAI monitor as a case study and find that measured false negative rate is highly sensitive to both the method used to generate attack policies and the level of optimisation applied within that method. This rate ranged from 0.5\% to 20.8\% across optimisation levels of a single method (reinforcement learning), and from 6.4\% to 23.0\% across methods at their best observed optimisation level (optimised prompting vs.\ transcript editing). Every attack policy was developed against proxy monitors only: all reported results show attacks transferring to the held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor. Our results suggest that monitor evasion evaluations can easily mislead: an evaluation using only basic attack methods may give false confidence that a monitor is strong. The sensitivity to attack-policy choice raises a problem: interpreting any particular result as evidence about deployment performance requires justification. The transcripts produced by attack policies are proxies for the dangerous behaviour a monitor must flag in deployment. These proxies can introduce visible artefacts, such as reasoning about a ``side task'' absent from the instructions shown to the monitor, that affect monitor judgements. Given the sensitivity above, the choice of proxy can change an evaluation's conclusion. Reading these results as evidence about deployment therefore requires a separate legitimacy argument for why the proxies are informative, including any justification of conservativeness. Making convincing arguments remains an open problem.

### 3. [Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces for Hard Math Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34928v1)
👤 Lukas Aichberger, Adam Golinski | 📅 2026-11-25

**详情:** Post-training large language models (LLMs) for mathematical reasoning often relies on accepted traces whose final answers pass a verifier. For hard math problems, however, such traces can be rare under independent Monte Carlo (MC) sampling. To address this, we propose Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces (DART), a trace-mining method that starts from a rejected trace, decomposes it into reasoning steps, and ranks them by position-weighted uncertainty. It then generates deliberately distinct alternative candidates for highly ranked steps using reference-answer guidance and a meta-thought before completing the resulting traces. On greedy-unsolved DeepMath-103K problems, DART recovers 37% more problems than MC sampling at a matched number of generated traces. Its estimated generation cost per recovered problem is nearly identical, while its cost per accepted trace is 30% lower. Post-training Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on traces mined by DART achieves the highest point estimate in five of six evaluations across MATH-500 and AIME benchmarks. These results demonstrate that revisiting rejected traces with DART can expand problem coverage and provide effective post-training data.

### 4. [$μ$-Bench: Benchmarking Multi-User Task Completion in Shared Interaction Voice Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34920v1)
👤 Qihao Lin, Guanxu Chen | 📅 2026-11-25

**详情:** Voice agents in shared spaces must satisfy interdependent multi-user requests while upholding per-user authority, privacy, and task constraints. Existing evaluations offer limited coverage when participants react throughout a session. We introduce $μ$-Bench, a benchmark for joint task completion in closed-loop, full-duplex multi-user voice interaction. Simulated participants act from isolated profiles of goals, knowledge, and permissions, adapting to the agent's speech and tool outcomes. Across 20 domains, 100 scenarios vary group size, acoustics, and follow-up demands to form 800 cases (408 tool-augmented, 392 tool-free). Joint Task Success (JTS) requires all user goals, privacy boundaries, and terminal states to hold jointly, while Pure JTS further disallows unauthorized tool calls; Attribution, Threading, and Coordination (ATC) diagnostics localize failures. Across 13 voice agents, proprietary systems peak at 62.24\% tool-free and 65.44\% tool-augmented success, while open-weight models remain below 20\%. ATC diagnostics show that open-weight models lag primarily in multi-turn attribution and threading, and controlled contrasts reveal that larger groups consistently degrade joint task success across capable systems. By coupling reactive multi-user simulation with verifiable session-level outcomes, $μ$-Bench provides a rigorous testbed for shared voice agents.
